# Mission

## Who

Nasarawa is a creative studio working in media. This site is their portfolio: the
primary place a prospective client, collaborator, or jury (awwwards and similar)
goes to understand what the studio makes and whether it's any good.

## Target audience

Four groups, each reading the site differently:

- **Prospective clients/brands** — evaluating the studio for commission. They
  want proof of capability and fit, fast: what does this studio make, is it
  good, is it right for my project. This is the primary commercial audience —
  when design decisions trade off against each other, clarity and credibility
  for this group wins.
- **Awwwards jury / design community** — judging craft and execution closely.
  They'll scrutinize interaction detail, typography, performance — the stuff
  that doesn't matter to a casual visitor but is the whole point for them.
- **Collaborators & talent** — directors, photographers, other studios or
  freelancers sizing up the studio as a potential partner or employer. Want a
  sense of the studio's sensibility and the caliber of people/work behind it.
- **Press / media industry** — trade press or outlets who might cover or
  feature the studio. Want a clear, quotable sense of who Nasarawa is and
  what makes their work notable.

The positioning below (image-led, subtle, editorial) is meant to satisfy all
four at once rather than picking one — a portfolio with enough craft to
impress a jury is also the clearest proof-of-capability for a client.

## v1 goal

Ship a focused portfolio: show the work, show who they are, make it easy to get
in touch. No shop, no blog, no lookbook yet — those are real, planned, and the
site should be built so they slot in later without a rebuild, but they are
explicitly **not** v1 scope. See `roadmap.md`.

## Positioning

An awwwards-caliber portfolio, built on craft rather than spectacle:

- **Image-led.** v1 is a grid of still work — photography, stills, branding,
  whatever the studio's output is — not video. Video is a real future direction
  (see roadmap) but is not a v1 dependency.
- **Subtle over heavy.** Motion exists to make the page feel considered, not to
  be the point. Text and images enter/exit with restraint — short fades, small
  translates, maybe a stagger. Nothing that fights the work for attention.
- **Editorial, not noisy.** Typography and spacing carry most of the visual
  weight. Grid of work is the hero, not a splash animation or a loading gate.

## What "winning" looks like for this site

- The work is legible and well-presented within seconds of landing.
- Scrolling feels smooth and intentional, not gimmicky.
- It's fast — image-heavy pages that still load and feel instant.
- It's honest about scope: a tight, polished v1 beats a sprawling, half-finished
  one. Depth (case studies, blog, shop, lookbook) is a deliberate *next* step,
  not something half-built into v1.

## Non-goals (for now)

- No e-commerce / shop.
- No blog / journal / news.
- No lookbook.
- No CMS-backed editing workflow (content is hardcoded for v1; see
  `tech-stack.md`).

These are deferred, not rejected — `roadmap.md` has them as named future phases
so decisions made in v1 don't foreclose them.
